A slider tool for comparing US and Canadian drug prices

A self-serve calculator that turns an abstract pricing claim into a number the visitor set themselves.

  • Interaction design
  • Data visualisation
  • Product design
View in Figma / Adobe XD ↗

The problem

People paying US prices for medication have a hard time picturing what the Canadian price actually saves them. A single stated number doesn't land the way an interactive comparison does.

The approach

I designed a two-panel slider calculator: drag to set your current US out-of-pocket spend, and see the equivalent Canadian price and the dollar difference update live, side by side, with no form to fill in first.

The slider also works with keyboard arrow keys and announces its updated value to screen readers on every change, so the live-update interaction doesn't come at the cost of accessibility. Numbers are formatted as currency as you drag, not left as raw digits, so the result reads as a real price rather than a placeholder value.

The outcome

Self-serve calculator Interactive pricing tool

A visitor who sets their own number believes the result more than one they're simply told.

Because the calculator needs no login and no form, it works as a standalone embed on any page that wants to make the pricing argument concrete.
