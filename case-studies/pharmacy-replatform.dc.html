Replatforming ten pharmacy storefronts off legacy .NET

The problem

A portfolio of ageing .NET sites was slow, inconsistent, and hard to trust — a real problem when someone is deciding whether to send you their medical details.

The approach

I led the end-to-end redesign onto WordPress: restructured the drug catalogue and search so people could find a medication by brand, generic, or condition, rebuilt checkout around visible trust signals, and brought every template to WCAG 2.1 AA.

Ran it as designer and project manager across concurrent client timelines, with sprint planning, design reviews, and retrospectives alongside developers and marketers.

Each migration started with a content and template audit of the existing .NET site, flagging what to carry forward, what to consolidate, and what to drop, before any new page got designed. Trust signals in checkout meant licensing and pharmacy credentials, clear delivery timelines, and a visible security badge placed at the moment of hesitation rather than buried in a footer. Running ten client timelines at once meant staggering releases so no two sites launched in the same sprint, with a shared review checklist to keep quality consistent across every client.

The outcome

+32% conversions Portfolio-wide

Consistent, accessible templates across all ten storefronts, with checkout and catalogue changes that carried straight through to conversion.

Because every site now shared the same template set, later improvements, a faster search, a clearer refill reminder, only had to be designed once and rolled out across the whole portfolio.
