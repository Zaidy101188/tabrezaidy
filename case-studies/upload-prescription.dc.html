Upload Prescription: removing the step that ended the order

A guided upload flow replacing the dead end customers hit when they had a valid paper prescription and no way to hand it over.

  • UX research

The problem

Customers with a valid paper prescription had no obvious way to hand it over, so they abandoned the cart and phoned the call centre — or didn't come back at all.

The approach

I mapped the full journey from drug page to fulfilment, interviewed support staff about the calls they fielded daily, and rebuilt the handoff as a single guided upload with clear file guidance, progress feedback, and a plain explanation of what happens next.

Carried it from research and flow mapping through high-fidelity prototypes, developer handoff, and QA on live traffic.

Support staff described the same pattern on nearly every call: a customer had already decided to buy, then hit a page with no clear next step, and gave up or picked up the phone instead. The redesigned flow replaced that dead end with three visible stages, upload, verification, and confirmation, each with its own status so a customer waiting on pharmacist review isn't left wondering if the upload even worked. File guidance (accepted formats, a legible-photo example, a size limit) sits right at the point of upload instead of in a help article three clicks away.

The outcome

+41% conversions Prescription orders

A step that used to end the order became one that people could complete on their own, in the flow, without a phone call.

The flow was built specifically to close off the reason customers were calling in, so that friction doesn't just resurface somewhere else in the journey.
