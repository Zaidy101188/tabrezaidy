A price-comparison tool for people paying out of pocket

A comparison widget that puts copay tier, coinsurance, cash price, and Canadian price side by side, for people on high-deductible plans who have no easy way to see what a medication actually costs.

The problem

Americans on high-deductible plans have no straightforward way to see what a medication actually costs them — copay tier, coinsurance, cash price, and the Canadian price all live in different places.

The approach

I'm designing a comparison widget over a structured dataset of around 21 common drugs that puts those four numbers side by side in one view.

The dataset needed a defensible structure before any interface could sit on top of it: each drug entry carries strength, common dosage, average commercial copay tier, typical coinsurance range, US cash price, and the equivalent Canadian price, sourced and dated so the comparison holds up to scrutiny rather than reading as a marketing number. The widget is designed to sit inline in an article rather than behind a lookup form, so someone reading about their condition sees their own drug's numbers without a separate search step.
