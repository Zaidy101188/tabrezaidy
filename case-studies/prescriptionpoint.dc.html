Prescription Point: making a cross-border pharmacy order feel safe

A Canadian and international online pharmacy where the hardest job isn't selling the price difference — it's proving, at every step, that the order is legitimate.

The problem

Ordering medication from a Canadian pharmacy is a real way to cut prescription costs, but it asks a first-time visitor to clear a lot of doubt before they'll hand over a prescription and a payment method: is this pharmacy actually licensed, is the medication the same drug, and why does checkout suddenly need an ID and a voided cheque?

Every one of those questions is legitimate — cross-border pharmacy is a regulated business — but a site that only presents them as compliance friction, without ever explaining why, loses the customer before the savings even land.

The approach

The homepage leads with proof, not price: a stats band (years in business, prescriptions filled, satisfaction rate) sits directly under the hero, and a dedicated "Why choose a licensed online pharmacy" section names the actual accreditations — CIPA, IPABC, Norton Secured — instead of a generic trust badge row.

Pricing itself does the persuading: brand and generic versions of the same medication sit side by side as matched pairs, so the savings are a direct visual comparison rather than a claim the copy has to make. And because the checkout process genuinely does require a prescription upload, an ID, and payment verification, I broke it into an explicit, numbered guide instead of leaving customers to discover each requirement mid-checkout.

The process, screen by screen

From a search box to a verified, shipped order

01 · Homepage

Earning trust before asking for a search term

Above the fold: a medication search and the core promise, "reduce your pharmacy drug costs, order from Canada." Immediately below: the numbers that back it up, then a wall of matched brand/generic drug cards, a customer-service band with a real phone line, the licensing section, a medication-type browse for people who don't yet know the drug name, curated health articles, FAQs, and third-party reviews — trust reinforced at every scroll stop, not just once at the top.

02 · Ordering guide

Turning a compliance process into a 7-step guide

"How to Order [Drug] from Canada" pages walk the exact path a customer will take, numbered so nothing feels sprung on them mid-checkout: select dosage, select quantity, add to cart, log in or create an account, place the order, then provide the required documents — prescription, ID, and payment details, each its own numbered sub-step — and finally a prescription-verification call to confirm and finalize.

Each step pairs a short explanation of what to do with a screenshot of the actual screen, and the dosage and quantity steps carry real pricing so the guide doubles as a cost calculator before anyone commits to an order.

03 · Login & register

An account exists because CIPA-certified pharmacies require one

Login and registration share one screen so a returning customer never lands on the wrong half of the page. The register form asks for exactly what's needed to manage a prescription and an order — name, phone, email, password — with explicit, separate consent checkboxes for newsletters, partner communications, and the customer agreement, so a customer can opt into the account without opting into marketing by default.

The outcome

The redesign treats every compliance requirement as something to explain rather than something to hide: certifications are named, not just badged; the checkout's document requirements are laid out before checkout begins; and pricing proves itself through direct comparison instead of a marketing claim.

A consistent style guide — Roboto Slab headings over Lato body text, a teal-and-cream palette carried from the top utility bar through every certification badge — keeps that trust-first tone the same whether a customer lands on the homepage, a drug-specific ordering guide, or the account screen.
