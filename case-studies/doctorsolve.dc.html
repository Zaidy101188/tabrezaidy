DoctorSolve: a component library for an online pharmacy

A full online pharmacy, from homepage to checkout to legal pages, built on one shared component library instead of one-off page designs.

The problem

An online pharmacy needed a full site, from homepage to checkout to legal pages, built on one consistent system rather than one-off page designs, with strict content rules (real delivery windows, no over-promising, a single shared FAQ pattern) that had to hold across every page.

The approach

I built the site as a component library first: more than 60 named, reusable pieces covering hero banners, medication cards, filters and sort, checkout, trust band, savings calculator, live chat launcher, FAQ accordion, and dozens more.

Full page templates, shop and product pages, drug and condition directories, blog, legal, and order confirmation, were then assembled from that shared set. Every page sits in edge-to-edge section bands with their own surface colour, never a single centered wrapper or floating cards on a bare canvas.

The content rules lived alongside the components, not in a separate document: a medication card component enforced a required strength and price format, a delivery-window component only accepted ranges the pharmacy could actually commit to, and the FAQ pattern reused the same question format everywhere it appeared. That meant a writer filling in a template couldn't accidentally over-promise or introduce an inconsistent tone, the structure did that enforcement for them.

The outcome

60+ components, 15+ templates One shared system

A content or accessibility fix now lands once, in the component, and every page that uses it inherits it, instead of being patched page by page.

The section-band structure also made the site easy to extend: a new condition directory or a new legal page slots into the same full-bleed rhythm instead of needing its own one-off layout decision.
