Product cards that turned health articles into a sales channel

A contextual product card embedded in editorial content, connecting condition research to the medication that treats it.

  • Component design
  • Content strategy
  • CRO
View in Figma / Adobe XD ↗

The problem

Editorial traffic was large and completely unmonetised — people researching a condition never met the medication that treated it.

The approach

I designed a contextual product card that sits inside the article body, matching the tone of the content rather than interrupting it: plain drug name, strength, price, and a single honest action.

Placement mattered as much as the card itself: it sits after the paragraph that names the medication, not at the top of the article where it would read as an ad, and only once per article so it never competes with itself. The single action is deliberately plain, "See price," not a discount badge or urgency copy, because the audience is already anxious about cost and a hard sell erodes the trust the article just built.

The outcome

New revenue line From editorial traffic

The pattern opened a mid-funnel touchpoint that hadn't existed before and now generates incremental revenue across the whole pharmacy portfolio.

It also became a template other content types reused, condition pages and FAQ answers now carry the same card where relevant, without needing a bespoke layout each time.
