01 — Overview

Medicine that has to feel private before it feels medical

The platform takes someone from a free online assessment to a licensed provider's review to a prescription treatment delivered to their door, without ever requiring a clinic visit. It also covers a second condition, hair loss, on the same underlying flow.

My scope covered the marketing landing pages, the medical intake assessment, treatment and dosage selection, checkout, and the account dashboard where a customer manages refills and messages their provider.

Designed for

Persona

A first-time patient

Researching alone, likely on a phone, likely at night

Goal — find out if this is legitimate and get treatment without talking to anyone in person.

Frustration — most sites either over-explain the condition in clinical language or hide behind vague marketing that answers nothing.

02 — Challenge

Discretion and medical credibility, at the same time

Tone

Too clinical reads cold; too soft reads like it isn't real medicine.

Intake length

A real medical history needs many questions; a long form loses people before question three.

Trust

No in-person doctor and no physical storefront to point to as proof this is legitimate.

Discretion

Packaging, billing descriptors and account language all needed to stay unremarkable.

03 — Research & insights

What made people trust an intake form

  1. 01

    One question per screen made a long medical history feel like a conversation instead of a form.

  2. 02

    A visible progress bar mattered more than the actual length — people tolerate more steps once they can see the end.

  3. 03

    Naming the provider who reviews the assessment, before asking for payment, did more for trust than any testimonial.

  4. 04

    Plain, muted colour read as more discreet than the bright clinical blue most competitors used.

"I wanted to know a real doctor was going to read this before I answered anything personal."

Paraphrased from user interviews

07 — Outcome

What changed for patients

What worked

Breaking the assessment into one question per screen, with the provider named up front, turned a form that felt like paperwork into something people were willing to finish.

What I would change

The dashboard was designed after the acquisition flow shipped. Designed alongside it, refills and provider messaging could have shared more of the same patterns.
