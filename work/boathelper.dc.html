01 — Overview

Two audiences, one object: the task

An owner posts a job against a specific boat — engine, painting, varnishing, electrical. Craftsmen in range see it, send offers, and one is accepted into an agreement. Work runs, messages stay attached to the job, the task closes.

Scope: owner home, boat profiles, task creation, task and offer listings, craftsman and owner profiles, agreements, chat for both sides, and market pricing.

Designed for

Persona

Diane, 51

Boat owner, weekend sailor

Goal — get a fair quote for engine work without calling five marinas.

Frustration — no visibility into who is qualified or what a job like hers should cost.

Persona

Reuben, 38

Marine maintenance craftsman

Goal — fill slow weeks with nearby jobs he can quote with confidence.

Frustration — no shopfront — work comes from word of mouth alone.

02 — Challenge

Nobody trusts a price they cannot compare

Price opacity

Quotes for the same hull job can differ by hundreds. Owners had no reference point before posting.

Vague briefs

A one-line request produces guesswork offers, which forces a second round of messages.

Two-sided empty state

Craftsmen leave without tasks; owners leave without offers. Both sides need to feel activity on day one.

Commitment

Accepting an offer is a money decision made on a phone. It needs a moment of its own, not a button in a list.

04 — Design decisions

Three decisions carried most of the product

01 — Market data on the home screen

Active task count, average task price and a six-month price trend by category sit above the fold for owners. It sets expectations before anyone types a number, and it proves the market is alive to a craftsman deciding whether to stay.

02 — Category before description

Task creation asks for the category first, then shapes the prompts and photo guidance around it. An engine job and a varnishing job need different evidence, and asking for it up front removes a round trip.

03 — The agreement is a screen

Accepting an offer opens a page that restates scope, price and dates and needs confirmation from both sides. Everything after it — chat, progress, completion — hangs off that record.

06 — Outcome & learnings

A marketplace is judged on completed jobs

Tasks receiving an offer

More tasks got a first offer instead of sitting open with no response.

Time to first offer

Owners heard back faster once market pricing set clear expectations.

Agreements reaching done

More agreements closed instead of stalling after the quote stage.

What worked

Putting market pricing on the home screen changed the tone of the whole app. Owners stopped treating offers as a negotiation trap and started treating them as a comparison.

What I would change

The craftsman side deserves its own dashboard rather than a mirror of the owner's. Their day is about pipeline and travel radius, not boats.
