01 — Overview

Operational software, judged by how fast a shift ends

Complex healthcare workflows were transformed into clearer operational experiences: dashboard, patient management, care tasks, scheduling, field routes, notifications and a companion mobile application.

Users were not shopping. They were repeating the same twenty actions all day, and every extra click was multiplied by a caseload.

Designed for

Persona

Nadia, 34

Care coordinator

Goal — see what is late, what is next, and who needs her, in one glance at the start of a shift.

Frustration — patients, tasks, schedules and routes spread across disconnected screens.

Persona

Tomás, 29

Field nurse

Goal — get his next visit, patient notes and an outcome log on his phone between stops.

Frustration — operational software built for a desk, not a moving day.

02 — Challenge

Four jobs, four disconnected screens, one shift

Context

Patient history lived one place, today's tasks another. Coordinators rebuilt context by memory.

Priority

Nothing distinguished an urgent task from a routine one until it was overdue.

Scheduling

Schedule and route were planned separately, so travel time was invisible until the day started.

Field use

Staff on the road were handed a desktop layout shrunk onto a phone.

03 — Research & workflow mapping

I mapped the shift before I mapped the screens

  1. 01

    The day starts with triage, not browsing. The landing screen had to answer "what needs me first".

  2. 02

    Most task updates are one field. They did not deserve a full-page form.

  3. 03

    Scheduling decisions are geographic. A route view had to sit beside the calendar.

  4. 04

    Field staff needed three things on mobile: next visit, patient notes, and a way to log the outcome.

“Tell me what is late, what is next, and who I am seeing. Everything else can wait.”

06 — Outcome & learnings

Fewer screens per task

Task completion

Coordinators cleared their task list without switching between screens.

Steps to update a task

Updating a task went from a multi-field form to a single tap.

Mobile adoption in the field

Field staff started logging visits from the app instead of calling them in.

What worked

Designing the triage screen first gave every later decision a test: does this help someone start their shift faster?

What I would change

I documented the component states after the first build rather than before it, which cost a round of rework with engineering.
