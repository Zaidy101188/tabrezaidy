01 — Overview

An e-commerce problem wearing healthcare constraints

The store sold prescription and over-the-counter medication to customers who were price-sensitive, often anxious, and mostly on a phone. Every page had to do two jobs at once: sell, and inform accurately enough that nobody ordered the wrong thing.

My remit covered the full journey — homepage, category, product detail, prescription upload, dosage and quantity selection, cart, checkout, savings calculator, trust content and the mobile experience.

Designed for

Persona

Priya, 42

Chronic prescription refiller

Goal — reorder her usual medication in under a minute without re-explaining dosage or history.

Frustration — comparing strengths and quantities across tabs because the product page would not let her compare in place.

Persona

Marcus, 27

First-time over-the-counter buyer

Goal — know whether a product needs a prescription before he commits to buying it.

Frustration — discovering the prescription requirement only after reaching the cart.

02 — Challenge

Simplify the journey without removing important healthcare information

Understanding

Product names, strengths and pack sizes read as a wall of near-identical options.

Pricing

Savings were the reason to buy, and they were the hardest thing on the page to find.

Prescriptions

Nothing on the page explained what a prescription requirement meant for the order.

Confidence

Buying medication online is a trust decision before it is a purchase decision.

07 — Outcome

What changed for customers

Prescription conversions

Stating the requirement early removed the checkout surprise that used to stall orders.

Checkout task completion

The single-column checkout let people finish without hunting for a missing field.

Support tickets on prescriptions

Fewer support conversations opened with "what does prescription required mean."

What worked

Moving the prescription requirement onto the product page meant nobody discovered it at checkout, and putting dosage, quantity and price in one block let people compare without leaving the page.

What I would change

The savings calculator was designed late, as an addition to the product page. Designed earlier, it could have shaped how the category page itself was structured.
