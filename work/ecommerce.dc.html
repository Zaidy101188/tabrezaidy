01 — Overview

Discovery, not decoration

Scope covered product discovery, navigation, search, filtering, product comparison, product detail, cart and checkout.

The redesign was structural. Almost none of the value came from restyling; it came from changing what the page lets you do next.

Designed for

Persona

Elena, 33

Returning shopper, price-sensitive

Goal — narrow a large catalogue without losing her place mid-browse.

Frustration — filters that reset every time she goes back to a product.

Persona

Jordan, 24

First-time visitor

Goal — compare a few options side by side before deciding.

Frustration — literal search results that force her to open a dozen tabs to compare.

03 — Problem

People could not hold a shortlist

Comparison happened in browser tabs because the site offered no way to keep two candidates side by side. Every comparison attempt was a chance to leave.

“I had four tabs open trying to work out which one was the same thing cheaper.”

01

Compare in place

02

05 — After

Comparison and checkout, shipped

06 — Outcome & learnings

What changed for shoppers

Add-to-cart rate

More product pages ended in an add-to-cart instead of a bounce.

Search exits

Fewer searches ended with someone leaving before applying a filter.

Checkout completion

More carts converted once checkout lost its surprise fields.

What worked

Putting filter state in the URL made shortlists shareable, which quietly turned comparison into a support tool as well as a sales one.

What I would change

The comparison table needed a mobile-native pattern earlier; the first attempt was a desktop table that had to be rethought.
