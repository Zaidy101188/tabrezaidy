01 — Overview

A bill, reframed as a balance sheet

Tenants already know the number and the date. What they don't have is a record: what they've paid this year, whether they've ever been late, what that reliability is worth. RentPay's home screen answers those before it asks for money.

Scope: onboarding and login, home dashboard, rent payment flow, payment confirmation, rewards, service add-ons (utilities, internet, cleaning, repairs) and payment history.

02 — Challenge

A transfer with no memory and no upside

No record

Bank transfers leave no trace a tenant can point to later — not to a landlord, not to a credit file.

No incentive

Paying on time and paying late look identical from the tenant's side. Nothing rewards the habit.

Scattered bills

Utilities, internet, cleaning and repairs sit in separate apps, separate logins, separate due dates.

Trust

This app moves someone's biggest monthly expense. It has to look and feel like a bank, not a startup.

04 — Design decisions

Three decisions that shaped the app

01 — The due-payment card leads the app

Amount, due date and a status pill sit in a white card against a blue field the instant the app opens. Nothing about balances, points or history is allowed to compete with it before the payment is made.

02 — Reliability is shown, not claimed

Paid-this-year, rewards earned and on-time-payments ratio sit directly below the fold as plain numbers. A 12/12 streak is a stronger trust signal than any badge or copy could be.

03 — Confirmation is a full, calm screen

A large green success state confirms the amount, the processing, and the points earned — a deliberate pause after a large transfer, before returning to the dashboard.

06 — Outcome & learnings

Trust is a design decision, repeated

On-time payment rate

More tenants paid before the due date instead of after it.

Time to complete a payment

Paying rent became a single screen instead of a multi-step form.

Rewards redemption

Tenants started redeeming points instead of letting them sit unused.

What worked

Leading with the paid-this-year and streak numbers, instead of hiding them in a profile tab, made the app feel like proof of good tenancy rather than another bill.

What I would change

Rewards currently only redeem toward add-on services. Letting points offset rent directly would close the loop the app is already promising.
