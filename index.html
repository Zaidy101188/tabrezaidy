05 — Design Process

Seven steps, in the order they earn their place

  1. 01

    Discover

    Read the analytics, the support tickets and the funnel before touching a canvas.

  2. 02

    Define

    State the problem in one sentence a stakeholder and a developer both accept.

  3. 03

    Explore

    Sketch flows and structure wide, cheap and fast — before pixels get expensive.

  4. 04

    Design

    Build the interface on system components so decisions stay consistent at scale.

  5. 05

    Test

    Prototype the risky part and watch someone try to finish the task.

  6. 06

    Refine

    Fix what the test exposed, then check states, edge cases and accessibility.

  7. 07

    Ship

    Hand off with specs, sit with the build, QA the release, then measure it.

06 — AI in Practice

How AI actually sits inside my process

Saying “I use AI” explains nothing. So here is one project, week by week, and exactly where the machine helped.

The project

A rent payment app built to make paying rent a tracked, rewarded habit. Two paragraphs of brief, one competitor link, one designer, and a build team waiting.

RentPay: Modernizing Rent Payments with Rewards →

  1. Day 1 — Interrogate the brief

    I argue with the model before I argue with the client

    I put the brief, the competitor notes and my own reading of the problem into a model and ask it to attack that reading. What is the weakest assumption here? What would a property manager say to this? It does not hand me the answer. It hands me the three questions I should have asked in the kickoff, and I take those to the client while it is still cheap to change direction.

  2. Day 2 — Widen before narrowing

    Twelve structures for one screen, ten of them discarded

    For the payment home screen I asked for a dozen different information hierarchies, each with a stated argument for why it leads. Most were obvious or wrong. One put the rewards balance above the fold — something I had filed as a later feature. That became the decision the whole product hangs on. The value was never the output. It was being forced past my first instinct in an hour instead of a week.

  3. Week 1 — Write the interface

    Copy stops being the thing I do last

    Labels, empty states, error messages, the sentence that explains why a payment is still pending. I draft all of it alongside the layout instead of pasting lorem ipsum and promising to come back. Then I cut it hard, because a model writes three sentences where the screen has room for one. The design reviews better when the words are real.

  4. Week 2 — Break it on purpose

    The edge cases I would have found in QA, found in Figma

    What does a tenant with no payment history look like? What happens when a payment fails after it is marked complete? What breaks at 200% text size, or in direct sunlight checking a receipt outside? I run the flows through a model as a checklist generator, then design the states that survive the list. It is unglamorous work that used to arrive as bug tickets.

  5. Week 3 — Make it clickable

    A real prototype in an afternoon, not a sprint

    I build working front-end prototypes with AI assistance, so testing happens on something people can actually use rather than a hotspot deck. Developers get a reference that answers the questions a static file cannot: what moves, what waits, what happens on failure. My HTML, CSS and React background is what makes that handoff honest.

  6. Throughout — Where I stop

    Four things I do not delegate

    • Deciding what problem is worth solving.
    • Talking to the people who will use it.
    • Taste — which of the twelve options is right.
    • Accountability for what ships.

AI shortens the distance between an idea and something you can react to. It does not decide which idea deserved the trip.
